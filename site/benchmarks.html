Benchmarks

What vci costs and what it saves, as measured. Every number says where and when it was taken, and links to the notes it comes from.

The chart on the overview is a model. This page has measurements only: production CI jobs of one large Rails suite, the plan step across fifteen releases, synthetic stores on a laptop, and a pilot against Turso's hosted service. Where two readings of the same thing disagree, both are here.

vex's Rails suite in production

ComposalAI/vex is a Rails application with 2,078 test files on 23 October 2026 (2,230 by the 25th), split over two CI jobs per pull request, with a seeded MySQL database and 16 test slots per job. Everything below is read from its GitHub Actions runs: the job logs, with vci's VCI_TIMING lines, and the jobs API for each step's start and end.

23 to 25 October: minutes per job

The first plain row is the median of 769 plain jobs in the week before vci took over, 13 to 19 October, as Measured on vex's Rails tests and CI is the bottleneck report it. The rows with vci are from the same pages, read from the logs of 23 to 25 October. A job where little changed was shorter than the plain job; a job where everything re-ran was longer, because each file runs in its own process so its inputs can be recorded, and about a thousand attestations are then signed, stored and pushed.

The plain figure did not hold up. Read again from the jobs API on 25 October (notes-rails-cost.md), the plain jobs of 16 and 17 October took a median of 272 s (application 261 s, services 284 s), and those of 18 October 304 and 318 s: 4.5 to 5.3 minutes, not 3.5. The 3.5 could not be reproduced. Since then the MySQL service image is pulled on every job, which takes container setup from 20 s to 48 s, plain or not.

Checking, signing and storing a run

After its tests, vci run checks each unit, signs its attestation and stores it. 2.8.0 signs in process (ed25519 and ECDSA key files, and through ssh-agent) instead of starting ssh-keygen per unit, and writes a project's units in one transaction instead of a commit per write. Measured on a synthetic module of 1,200 Go packages, on a 14-core Mac busy with other work, medians of three runs, on 25 October 2026 (2.8.0 release notes):

The units are still checked one per CPU at a time: twice as many threads as cores grew the check's thread-seconds by half and did not shorten the step. On a heavily loaded machine, SQLite wrote a 1,200-unit run's 2,400 records in 4.8 s with a commit each, and in 0.9 s as one transaction.

Turso's hosted service from a GitHub runner

On 27 October 2026 a pilot ran vci 2.8.0 against Turso's hosted service in aws-eu-west-1, about 25 ms of TCP round trip from vex's runners. It took three workflow runs (1, 2, 3), each on one composal-x64-8x runner, the label vex's Rails jobs use, with a fresh namespace per run. The project was vex-api's 2,291 Rails units. The git-meta side is vex's production path, from the 38 pull-request jobs of rails-tests.yml that ran between 00:12 and 01:50 UTC that day, mostly on vci 2.7.36.

Checking, signing and storing about 810 Rails unitsSeconds
Turso, into a new namespace (four requests of up to 8 MiB, 3 to 11 s each)21.6 to 30.4
Turso, into a namespace already holding 1,600 to 2,500 attestations39.8 to 41.6
git-meta's local store, in vex's jobs on the same host class9.3 to 18, then vci push: a median of 22 (up to 94)

Turso gains the most in the plan: no cache to restore, nothing to fetch, and only the attestations a plan checks cross the network. Without the cache, on the pilot's own runners, git-meta's fetch and plan took 86 to 94 s. Storing is slower, by about what the push it replaces costs, so the write side comes out roughly even. A replica gained nothing on a fresh runner: synced from nothing, its plans took 4.1 to 5.1 s, and its first sync reads the whole namespace, about a millisecond a record, where a remote plan reads only what it checks. It suits a machine that keeps its clone. The pilot wrote 4,914 attestations with none refused.

After the pilot, vex moved its Rails CI to Turso, in a pull request that is still under review.

Three runs and a small namespace. The largest pilot namespace held 3,276 attestations. vex's git-meta holds about 24,000, and its plans check 3,700 to 26,000 candidates; a remote plan's cost grows with the values it reads, so Turso at that size is not measured. Turso's latency varied up to 3x: the same read of 1,638 values took 2.5 to 7.8 s. The git-meta figures are whole CI steps of other jobs, from the jobs API, and the Turso ones vci plan's wall time in the pilot. On the pilot's host class (257,583 MiB of memory) the git-meta median was 51.5 s.
